Learners ready for a challenge

Students preparing for exams

Use subject tuition to work on understanding and exam technique. Confirm the qualification, specification and any separate exam arrangements first.

Learning together

A clear role for the teacher, learner and parent

Agree the balance of live teaching, practice and adult support at the beginning. Review it as the learner becomes more independent.

During a live lesson

The teacher explains and models a task, then asks the learner to try and explain their thinking. Questions and attempts help show whether to revisit an idea or move forward. Have paper and subject equipment ready, with a way to share written work.

Between lessons

Agree which practice to attempt and how feedback will be shared. A parent can help protect study time and note where support was needed. For younger children, discuss whether an adult should be nearby to help with the device and instructions.

For a manageable record, keep one independent attempt and one question for the next lesson. Our home education planning guide shows how this can work over several weeks.

Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

Keep a learning portfolio that shows decisions, not just finished pages

A useful home-learning record can show how a learner’s understanding develops and what needs teaching next. It need not contain every activity or become an exercise in presentation.

A practical example

Keep a dated original attempt, a short note about the explanation or practice that followed and a later independent example. Add the learner’s own comment about what changed. Use the record to discuss the next subject priority with the tutor.

Choose a manageable amount of evidence and avoid sharing unnecessary personal information. Tuition can support selected subjects and learning goals; confirm the scope, resources and arrangements directly. This portfolio idea is a learning tool, not advice that a particular record fulfils any legal or examination requirement.

Agree how an independent attempt will be recorded

For one agreed learning goal, keep a dated attempt made without a live model or step-by-step adult help. Record the resources available and any support given, then discuss the work with the tutor.

A small, clear record can be more informative than a large collection of completed pages whose conditions are unknown. Use it to plan teaching, not to claim an official grade or fulfilment of a legal requirement. Confirm any formal assessment or examination arrangements with the relevant provider separately from the tuition plan.

Before you book

Questions families ask

We can talk through the details of your child's plan before you decide whether online tuition is the right fit.

Is this a full-time online school?

This service offers online subject tuition and support for your family's home-education plan. It is not offered as a complete full-time school programme. We agree the subjects, lesson frequency and resources with you. In England, parents choosing home education remain responsible for the overall education; the GOV.UK home-education guidance explains the starting points.

Can we book daytime lessons or several subjects?

Tell us the days, times and subjects you are considering. We will check tutor availability and discuss whether we can offer a suitable regular plan. There is no fixed school-day timetable or assumption that every subject is available at every time.

Can my child stay at school and use this service?

Yes. Online tuition can support a pupil who attends school, whether they need help with a current topic, a stronger study routine or exam preparation. Lessons can follow the course being taught at school.

Are lessons live or self-paced?

Teaching Success offers live online subject lessons at agreed times. Independent practice can support those lessons, with the resources and feedback agreed as part of your plan. This page does not describe a self-paced course subscription.

Can the teaching pace change as my child progresses?

Discuss what the learner can do independently and what still needs explanation. That helps the tutor decide which ideas to revisit and what to introduce next. Lesson times and frequency remain subject to the arrangement agreed with you.

What equipment do we need?

A laptop or tablet with a stable internet connection, microphone and a way to show work is a useful starting point. Have paper, a pen and any subject equipment ready. We confirm the lesson platform and practical setup before the first session, including what adult support a younger child may need.

Are exams and exam-centre fees included?

Exam entries, exam-centre arrangements and fees are separate from tuition. For a home-educated learner taking qualifications, confirm the board, specification and an appropriate centre before committing to a course. Ask the centre about any practical, speaking or coursework requirements.

How much will the lessons cost?

The price depends on the subject, level and agreed lesson arrangement. Send us the support and frequency you are considering; we will confirm the cost and availability before you book. A tailored enquiry is not an enrolment or payment commitment.
