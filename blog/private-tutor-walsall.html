Check whether online help transfers back to the exercise book

A successful online session should leave the pupil better able to work away from the call. Build a small paper task into the follow-up.

A practical example

After a shared example, ask the learner to complete a related question in their normal exercise book without a screen prompt. At the next session, show the original attempt and explain where support was or was not needed.

If the task depended on the tutor’s on-screen layout, practise reconstructing the useful structure on paper. Confirm the subject, level and availability with Kevin and Serena. Teaching Success’s in-person base is Smethwick; this page offers a way to discuss online support, not a separate Walsall teaching branch.

Agree what an online lesson’s independent work looks like

Before the session, clarify how the learner will show work: a shared board, paper held to a camera or another agreed method. Choose an approach that lets the tutor see the reasoning without collecting unnecessary information.

Keep one independent attempt at the end and compare it with the lesson’s model. This gives both sides a concrete review point. Confirm subject coverage and availability directly; the online arrangement should not be read as a promise of a Walsall branch or a tutor travelling to the home.

Show where help was needed

A completed homework page can hide several adult explanations. Put a small mark beside questions completed independently and a different mark beside those requiring a hint. Record the hint briefly: identifying the formula is different from correcting arithmetic.

Ask the student to retry one supported question with different numbers or wording. Bring both attempts to the tutoring conversation. They give the tutor more useful evidence than a score alone.

Agree a starting point, then check it again

For maths, the immediate goal might be choosing a method for a ratio question. For English, it might be linking a quotation to a clear explanation. For science, it might be showing units and substitutions in a calculation. Confirm which subjects and levels are currently available before arranging a lesson.

Choose one goal to review after an agreed block of lessons. Compare a fresh task with the first attempt: accuracy matters, but so do explanation, recall and the amount of prompting needed.

Questions to ask before committing

  • Who will teach the lessons, and do they cover this subject and level?
  • How will written work be shared and checked online?
  • What are the fee, cancellation arrangements and available times?
  • What practice is realistic between lessons, and how will progress be reviewed?
