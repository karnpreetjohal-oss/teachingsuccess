Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

Bring a question that reveals the sticking point

A first tuition conversation is more useful when it starts with an attempted task. An untouched worksheet shows the topic; the pupil’s working shows where help may be needed.

A practical example

Choose one recent question and mark the last step the learner understands. Keep any crossings-out and teacher comments. Ask whether the missing connection is knowledge, interpretation or choosing a method, rather than describing the whole subject as difficult.

Agree one outcome for the next lesson, such as explaining a particular step without a hint. Confirm the tutor, subject and lesson format before booking. The evidence should guide the plan, while the learner’s own explanation remains part of the conversation.

Give a tuition enquiry a clear starting point

A useful first message can be short: year group, subject, the part of learning that is difficult and whether online or Smethwick-based lessons are being considered. Add the exam board or tier only where relevant.

For example, explain that a pupil can calculate a fraction of an amount but struggles when the whole is missing. That is more actionable than “needs to improve maths”. Leave pupil records and sensitive documents out of the initial enquiry, and confirm the appropriate tutor and available arrangements directly.
