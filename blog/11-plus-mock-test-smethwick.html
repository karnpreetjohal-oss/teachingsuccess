What It Checks

Six things a good mock test reveals.

Two children can score similarly and need completely different next steps. The mock should show not just where marks were lost — but why.

01

English under pressure

Reading accuracy and answer quality often drop once a clock is running. The mock shows whether comprehension holds up when it has to.

02

Maths speed and accuracy

Are errors from method gaps — or from rushing? A timed paper separates the two clearly.

03

Verbal reasoning

Vocabulary, pattern recognition and instruction-following all tested together. The mock shows whether this area is currently strong enough.

04

Non-verbal reasoning

Many children understand the concepts but lose marks through rushing or spending too long on the wrong question type.

05

Stamina across a full paper

Does concentration hold across all sections, or does it fade? A full sitting is the only way to find out.

06

A clear next step

Which papers to prioritise, how much timed practice is needed, and whether tuition needs to change — answered after one sitting.

When To Book

Book while there's still time to act on the result.

The sweet spot is Year 5 or the summer before Year 6. A mock test too late in the process can still help — but only if there is enough time to change the plan.

1

Early baseline

Year 5, once preparation has started. Confirms whether the current level is promising or whether a bigger push is needed earlier.

2

Midway check

Usually the strongest time. Enough time left to reshape tuition, practice habits and school-route expectations based on what the mock shows.

3

Pre-exam review

Close to the real exam. Shows whether the child is settled under timed pressure — or whether one final push will make a difference.

FAQ

Common questions before booking.

What should a mock test include?

Timed work across the real exam sections — English, maths, verbal and non-verbal reasoning — plus feedback that explains the result, not just a score.

When is the right time to book?

Year 5 or the summer before Year 6. Early enough to act on the result. A mock right before the exam is still useful if there's time to make changes.

Can a mock test predict the real result?

No. It shows current readiness and where marks are being lost. That's what makes it useful — a clear picture now, while there's still time to change things.

Do you give feedback afterwards?

Yes. A qualified teacher reviews the result with you — which sections are secure, where the gaps are, and what to focus on next.
