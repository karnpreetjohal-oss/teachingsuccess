What a Year 3 tutor should really be helping with

Year 3 support should focus on moving from early basics into longer reading, fuller writing and more deliberate working. At this stage, the aim is usually to make school feel steadier week by week rather than to pile on extra work without a plan.

Where Year 3 pupils usually start to wobble

The common pressure point in Year 3 is when Key Stage 2 expectations rise faster than confidence. A strong plan makes those problems visible early, then works through them calmly before they grow into a bigger Key Stage 2 issue.

What the first half-term of Year 3 tuition should include

  • Short checks on reading fluency, vocabulary growth and writing answers in complete sentences so the tutor can see what is secure and what still needs direct teaching
  • Regular work on times-table foundations, written methods and choosing the right operation in word problems rather than waiting until the next school test exposes the gap again
  • A simple weekly routine that parents and pupils can actually maintain
  • Enough review to build confidence, not just enough to finish the worksheet
