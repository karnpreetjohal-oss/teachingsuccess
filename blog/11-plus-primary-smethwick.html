Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

Keep a balanced week when one section feels easier

Children may gravitate towards the practice they already enjoy. A balanced routine needs room for a weaker area without making every session feel difficult.

A practical example

Choose one familiar task and one small challenge from different areas, such as a short reading passage and a visual-pattern problem. Review the challenge with an explanation, then finish with a related attempt the child can complete independently.

Revisit the balance as evidence changes rather than assigning identical time to every section indefinitely. Check the child’s intended entry year and current official guidance before planning test preparation. If their entrance test has already taken place, agree a new goal around school learning instead of continuing a final-test countdown.

Choose one skill to revisit across two subjects

A child learning to follow multi-step instructions can practise that skill in both a short maths task and a reading activity. Keep each subject task familiar so the instruction sequence is the feature being tested.

Ask the child to restate the steps before beginning and check which step was missed if the answer goes wrong. This does not replace subject teaching or establish general readiness from one activity. It gives the family a specific observation to discuss while preserving a balanced routine around ordinary primary-school learning.

Your next step

Plan for your child's year group and entry year

Preparation should fit the time available and the skills your child needs. Start with the intended school entry year rather than following a countdown meant for a different cohort.

01

Year 4: secure the foundations

Build reading habits, vocabulary and reliable number methods. Use short reasoning activities to notice how your child explains an answer. See our Year 4 preparation guide.

02

Year 5: make practice manageable

Balance maths, English and reasoning across the week. Review errors before adding more timed work. Try the Year 5 weekly plan.

03

If the entrance test is finished

Agree a fresh learning goal around current schoolwork, reading or maths confidence. Primary tuition can support the rest of Year 6 without continuing a pre-test countdown.

04

Before choosing tuition

Bring the intended entry year, any official test information and a recent example of work. The 11+ readiness checklist can help organise the discussion; it is not a pass prediction.

Questions parents ask

11 Plus tuition FAQs

When should 11 Plus preparation start?

Many families begin in Year 4 or Year 5. The best starting point depends on the child's current level and the time available before the test.

Do you teach all four 11 Plus areas?

Yes. Tuition can cover Maths, English, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning as part of one structured plan.

Do students complete mock papers?

Yes. Timed practice and mock-style papers are introduced when the student has built enough knowledge to benefit from them.

Which grammar schools do local families target?

Smethwick and Birmingham families often consider West Midlands grammar schools, including King Edward VI schools. Admission arrangements and dates should always be checked directly with the relevant consortium or school.
