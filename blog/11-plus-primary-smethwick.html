What 11 plus tuition in Smethwick has to cover

A useful 11+ plan is not just a pile of practice papers. For Smethwick families, the real task is to build four strands together: reading comprehension, maths fluency, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning. If one strand is ignored, mock scores can look unpredictable and children often lose confidence because they cannot see exactly what is improving.

Year 4, Year 5 and Year 6 need different pacing

Year 4 is usually about foundations: reading accuracy, number confidence and puzzle-style thinking. Year 5 is where preparation becomes more deliberate, with weekly reasoning practice and regular review of weak question types. Year 6 should be much more precise: timed work, mock-test feedback, careful correction and calm exam routines rather than last-minute content cramming.

What strong 11+ preparation should include

  • Reading practice that builds inference, vocabulary and stamina, not just speed.
  • Maths work that keeps KS2 method secure before moving into trickier reasoning.
  • Verbal reasoning taught by pattern type so children know what to look for.
  • Non-verbal reasoning strategies for rotation, reflection, sequences and matrices.
  • Mock-test review that turns scores into a specific next lesson, not a vague warning.

How this differs from a mock-test page

This page is the teaching route. It explains what your child needs to learn and practise each week. The mock-test pages are for checking performance under exam conditions. Both matter, but they do different jobs: tuition builds the skills, mock tests reveal whether those skills are ready under time pressure.
