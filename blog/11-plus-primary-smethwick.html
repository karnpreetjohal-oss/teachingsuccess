4 September 2026 update

Eight days to the Birmingham 11 Plus test

School has restarted and the King Edward VI Foundation entrance test is on Saturday 12 September. The challenge now is fitting light, precise preparation around a full school day without letting one difficult paper unsettle the final week.

01

Choose one final paper

Complete it early enough to review every guessed, incorrect and unanswered item; the review is where the remaining value sits.

02

Practise answer transfer

Use a separate answer sheet, two HB pencils and an eraser so the practical routine feels ordinary before test day.

03

Shorten after school

Use 15 to 20 minutes on one repeated error after a school day, then stop. Tired volume is not the same as useful practice.

04

Close the checklist

Confirm the allocated session, journey and permitted items with the 11+ readiness checklist, then resist adding a brand-new resource.

Final-week rhythm

A calm seven-day 11 Plus cycle

One final full paper can help if it is followed by careful review. Repeating full mocks every day usually leaves too little time to understand mistakes or recover mentally.

01

Days 1–2

Complete one realistic paper or paired timed sections, then spend the next session reviewing every uncertain answer.

02

Days 3–4

Teach the two most repeated error types and use short sets to check that the correction transfers.

03

Days 5–6

Use light mixed practice, reading and vocabulary review. Avoid introducing unfamiliar question books.

04

Day 7

Rest, prepare the practical details and keep family conversation away from scores and predictions.

Questions parents ask

11 Plus tuition FAQs

When should 11 Plus preparation start?

Many families begin in Year 4 or Year 5. The best starting point depends on the child's current level and the time available before the test.

Do you teach all four 11 Plus areas?

Yes. Tuition can cover Maths, English, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning as part of one structured plan.

Do students complete mock papers?

Yes. Timed practice and mock-style papers are introduced when the student has built enough knowledge to benefit from them.

Which grammar schools do local families target?

Smethwick and Birmingham families often consider West Midlands grammar schools, including King Edward VI schools. Admission arrangements and dates should always be checked directly with the relevant consortium or school.
