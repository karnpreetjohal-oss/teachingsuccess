Make parent feedback specific enough to act on

A parent update is most useful when it distinguishes effort, understanding and independence. “Worked hard” is encouraging, but it does not explain what to practise next.

A practical example

Ask for one example of a skill completed independently, one point still requiring a prompt and one manageable follow-up task. If a calculation was correct only after a hint, record that honestly rather than reporting the topic as finished.

Review the follow-up at the next lesson and adjust it if it was too difficult or too easy. West Bromwich families can use this framework when discussing support with the Smethwick-based team; agree the format and communication arrangements before the lessons begin.

Ask for feedback that names the next independent action

Compare “good progress today” with “can now identify the denominator; next, explain why the parts must be equal”. The second statement gives the family something observable without requiring a long written report.

Ask how the next action connects to the lesson and how much support is appropriate at home. If a parent is expected to teach an unfamiliar method, clarify that expectation rather than guessing. Feedback is most usable when the learner, family and tutor understand the same small next step.
