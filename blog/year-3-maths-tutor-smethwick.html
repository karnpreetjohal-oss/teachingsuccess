What Year 3 Maths covers

Year 3 is the first year of formal written methods. Children extend place value to 1,000, learn column addition and subtraction with carrying and exchanging, tackle the 3, 4 and 8 times tables, begin multiplying and dividing formally, work with tenths and simple fractions, and tell the time to the minute.

Where Year 3 students most often need help

The most common issue is a child who could get answers mentally in Year 2 now resisting the formal layout — and losing marks because the working is untidy or the place-value columns drift. Times-table gaps also start to slow multiplication and division right down.

The assessment that shapes Year 3

There is no national test in Year 3, which makes it an ideal year to fix foundations quietly before the stakes rise. The one date to keep in view is the Year 4 Multiplication Tables Check the following June, so we start building table fluency now.

What a weekly Year 3 Maths plan looks like

  • Secure column addition and subtraction with exchanging, set out clearly
  • The 3, 4 and 8 times tables to support formal multiplication and division
  • Understanding fractions as parts of a whole, including tenths
  • Telling the time to the minute on analogue and digital clocks

How lessons work

From there, weekly one-hour sessions mix direct teaching on the current priority with worked examples, independent practice and a short review of earlier topics so nothing slips. Lessons are taught by qualified teachers in Smethwick and Birmingham, or online across the UK, from £15 an hour.

Looking ahead

Year 3 lays the groundwork for the Multiplication Tables Check in Year 4, so we make table fluency a steady weekly habit rather than a last-minute push.
