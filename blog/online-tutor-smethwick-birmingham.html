What online tuition should really do

Online tuition works best when it is chosen for the right reasons: less travel, easier scheduling, stronger consistency and a pupil who can engage well through a screen. It is not automatically better or worse than in-person; it is a different route.

What online tuition should include

  • Consistency across busy weeks when travel would otherwise break the routine
  • Particular value for older students, exam classes and families slightly further from the Smethwick base
  • Live annotation, worked examples and paper review without losing lesson pace
  • The option to switch between formats when the year gets busier

When this route is the right fit

This route is strongest when the student is old enough to work independently on screen and the family wants to remove unnecessary travel friction.

Mid-August 2026: test online tuition before the school-night timetable begins

An online trial during the holiday can check the platform, camera position, writing setup and attention before the family relies on it every week. The teaching should still use marked work, live questioning and clear follow-up rather than becoming a video call with worksheets.

  • Test the desk and device: Make sure the tutor can see working and the pupil can annotate or share answers easily.
  • Remove avoidable friction: Check audio, links, charging and where lesson files will be stored.
  • Use a real teaching task: The trial should include explanation, independent practice and correction.
  • Choose the real slot: Confirm that the time works once travel, dinner, clubs and homework return.
