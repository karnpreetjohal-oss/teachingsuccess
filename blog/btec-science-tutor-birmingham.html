How BTEC Science is assessed

BTEC Applied Science splits assessment between internally assessed units (coursework-style assignments marked in school) and externally assessed units (timed exams set by Pearson). The balance varies by specification, but students typically sit two or three external exams and complete four to six internal units over the course of the qualification. Struggling students often need help with both — understanding the science content for external exams and structuring assignment responses to meet the Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria for internal units.

Internal units — what good assignment work looks like

BTEC assignment tasks use specific command words that carry precise meanings in the mark scheme — describe, explain, analyse, evaluate and justify each require different levels of response. Many students write at Pass level when the task requires Merit or Distinction standard, not because they lack the science knowledge, but because they have not been shown how to extend their answers appropriately. Kevin works through assignment briefs with students, identifying what each criterion requires and showing how to structure a response that meets the higher bands.

External units — where BTEC science meets exam technique

The externally assessed units in BTEC Applied Science require the same kind of exam preparation as GCSE science — understanding core content, applying it to unfamiliar contexts and answering extended questions precisely. The content in these units often covers Biology, Chemistry and Physics principles at a level similar to GCSE Triple Science. Kevin's science background means he can support students across all three discipline areas within the BTEC framework, not just one subject.

Level 2 versus Level 3 BTEC Science

Level 2 BTEC Applied Science is broadly equivalent to two GCSEs in science and is taken in Years 10 and 11. Level 3 is a post-16 qualification taken in sixth form or college, equivalent to one or two A-Levels. Level 3 is often chosen by students who want a science background for health, sport science, nursing or forensic science degrees without sitting the traditional A-Level route. The demands of Level 3 are substantially higher and the external exams in particular require a strong understanding of underpinning science concepts.
