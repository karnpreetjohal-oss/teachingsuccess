Tuition for Students with Additional Learning Needs in Smethwick

Teaching Success works with students who find the standard classroom pace difficult to follow — whether due to dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD tendencies, processing difficulties or simply a learning style that does not suit traditional teaching. This is not a clinical or therapeutic service, but Kevin and Serena have extensive experience adapting how they teach to match how individual students actually learn.

What adapted tuition looks like in practice

For students with dyslexia, sessions use larger text, verbal explanations alongside written ones, and avoid long unbroken reading tasks. For students who struggle with attention and sustained focus, lessons are structured in shorter activity blocks with clear transitions — typically 12 to 15 minutes per task with a brief change of pace in between. For students with dyscalculia, the emphasis is on concrete representations of number before moving to abstract methods, and no assumptions are made about what should already be automatic.

How sessions are planned for students with additional needs

  • The first session includes a more detailed conversation with parents about what has and has not worked in school
  • Lesson plans are shorter and more modular than standard sessions
  • Progress is tracked in smaller increments so success is visible more frequently
  • Parent communication after sessions is more detailed — explaining what was covered and what to watch for at home
  • Homework tasks are shorter and more targeted, with explicit instructions written down rather than given verbally

Subjects most commonly supported

English reading and writing support is the most frequent request, particularly for students with dyslexia who are struggling to keep up with comprehension tasks and extended writing at secondary level. Maths catch-up for students with dyscalculia is the second most common, often focused on number confidence, times tables, and written methods. Science at GCSE level also comes up frequently, where the vocabulary load and extended writing demands create barriers that targeted support can significantly reduce.

Working alongside school support

Many students receiving one-to-one tuition also have SENCO support at school or an EHCP. Kevin and Serena are happy to be aware of what is in a student's plan and to align tuition targets where appropriate — without overcomplicating the session or duplicating what the school is already doing well. The tuition should complement, not conflict with, any formal support already in place.
