What non-verbal reasoning tuition should cover

Non-verbal reasoning improves when children understand the logic behind each question type, not when they guess faster. A good programme breaks the paper into recognisable patterns, trains the pupil to eliminate weak options quickly and gradually increases pace once accuracy is secure.

  • Pattern families such as rotation, reflection, codes and shape movement
  • Error analysis so repeated traps are spotted and corrected early
  • Short timed sets to build speed without losing method
  • Linked maths, vocabulary and comprehension work to support the full 11+ profile

A weekly structure that lifts scores

  1. Lesson start: quick retrieval on two or three recent pattern types.
  2. Teaching block: one new non-verbal question family modelled step by step.
  3. Timed application: short set under light pressure with instant review.
  4. Home task: small follow-up pack so the method is reinforced between sessions.

How parents know the plan is working

You should see fewer careless mistakes, better pacing and clearer explanations from your child when they talk through an answer. If reasoning is improving but maths or reading are still weak, it helps to combine this with broader 11+ and KS2 preparation or targeted primary English and maths support.
