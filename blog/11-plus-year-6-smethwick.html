The Urgency of Year 6 — Without the Panic

Year 6 is the crunch year. For Smethwick children targeting grammar school entry — whether to the King Edward's Foundation schools in Birmingham, Handsworth Grammar School, or Sutton Coldfield Grammar School for Girls — the entry assessment takes place in September, just weeks after the new school year begins. This means the critical preparation window is the summer term of Year 5 through to the summer holidays, not Year 6 itself.

Kevin and Serena Johal work with Year 6 families who need focused, efficient preparation in the time remaining. The priority shifts in Year 6 from building new skills to consolidating and applying what has been developed — moving from individual topic work to full practice papers under timed conditions. The goal is for children to arrive at the exam feeling practised, calm and genuinely ready.

Full Practice Papers — Timed and Reviewed

In Year 6, practice papers become a central part of preparation. A child who has only ever worked through textbook exercises will find the pacing and format of a full timed assessment stressful — the time pressure, the varied question types, the need to keep moving and not get stuck on one problem. Kevin works with Year 6 students on paper-sitting technique: how to scan a paper before starting, how to use time wisely across sections, when to skip a question and come back, and how to manage the final minutes effectively.

After each paper, sessions focus on structured review — not just marking correct and incorrect but understanding why errors happened. Pattern errors (consistently losing marks on fraction questions or confusing angle rules) are identified and corrected. Individual questions the student found difficult are revisited until the underlying concept is secure, not just the answer memorised.

Mental Maths and Arithmetic Speed

Arithmetic speed is often the quiet difference between a competitive score and a missed place. 11+ Maths papers are time-pressured, and children who cannot calculate quickly — without reaching for written methods on every question — run out of time on harder problems. Kevin builds mental maths speed through targeted daily practice activities and specific strategies for each operation:

  • Multiplying by 9, 11, 25, 50 and 100 using mental shortcuts
  • Quick fraction-to-percentage conversions (1/8, 1/5, 3/4, etc.)
  • Estimation to check plausibility of answers quickly
  • Number bonds and complements to 100, 1000 and beyond
  • Carrying addition and subtraction mentally in multi-digit calculations

Reading Speed and Comprehension Under Pressure

The English and verbal reasoning components of grammar school entry tests require children to read and process text quickly and accurately. Serena Johal works with Year 6 pupils on reading efficiency — not speed-reading gimmicks, but genuine strategies for extracting relevant information from a passage quickly, identifying the key inference the examiner is testing, and writing a concise, precise answer rather than an essay.

She also works on the anxiety element: many children who read well in relaxed conditions find exam conditions disruptive. Session practice in timed reading helps normalise the experience, so that by the time the exam arrives it feels like something the child has done many times before — which they have.
