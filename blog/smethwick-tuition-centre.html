Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

Use the first visit to test a learning routine

A first visit can show how a learner settles, asks for help and moves between explanation and independent practice. Those observations are useful alongside a subject assessment.

A practical example

Bring a recent task and let the learner attempt a short related question after discussion. Notice whether the instructions are clear, the workspace is usable and the pupil knows what to do when stuck. Discuss any practical support that would make participation easier.

Before agreeing further sessions, confirm the location, timing, format and next learning goal. A welcoming first visit matters, but the most useful outcome is a shared understanding of what will be taught and how the learner will have opportunities to use it independently.

Bring the resource that matches the current classroom method

For an initial in-person lesson, a current exercise book or school-provided example can show the notation and method the learner is expected to use. Select a relevant page rather than bringing every folder.

Explain which parts were completed independently and where help was given. This gives the tutor a more reliable starting point and reduces avoidable confusion from introducing a different method too early. Confirm the appointment and materials beforehand, and share only the information needed for the lesson.
