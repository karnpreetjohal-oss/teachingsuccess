Probability without the missing outcome

A probability calculation depends on a complete, appropriate set of outcomes. A tidy fraction can still be wrong if the list underneath it is incomplete.

A practical example

For two fair six-sided dice, make a 6 by 6 outcome grid. A total of 7 occurs in six ordered pairs, so its probability is 6/36 = 1/6. Listing only the totals from 2 to 12 incorrectly treats totals with different numbers of combinations as equally likely.

Try a different total and explain why the grid is the useful model. Bring the attempt to an online lesson so the tutor can see the enumeration, not just the final fraction. Current lesson format and tutor availability should be confirmed with the Smethwick-based team.
