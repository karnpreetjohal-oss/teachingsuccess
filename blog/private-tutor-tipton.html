Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

Reduce copying so the thinking becomes visible

Copying a long question can consume time without revealing much about understanding. In a tutoring task, it can help to separate recording demands from the subject goal.

A practical example

Provide a printed or clearly displayed question and ask the learner to annotate the important information, choose a method and show the working. Compare that with a shorter task where writing itself is the learning objective.

Discuss which demand should be practised rather than removing all writing automatically. Use the learner’s school expectations and any agreed support as context. Tipton families should confirm the current lesson format with the Smethwick-based team; the focus is a suitable arrangement, not a claim of a local branch.

Reduce a crowded task without removing its thinking

Cover the later questions on a busy worksheet and show one at a time. Keep the wording and mathematical demand unchanged so the pupil can focus on the same intended skill.

If performance improves, note the effect of the presentation rather than immediately claiming the topic is secure. Try another example and discuss the observation with the tutor. Reducing visual clutter is different from simplifying the concept, and keeping that distinction clear helps avoid accidentally changing what the activity is meant to reveal.
