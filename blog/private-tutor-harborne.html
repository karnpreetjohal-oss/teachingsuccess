Let the learner write the lesson handover

A short handover written by the pupil can reveal whether the main idea survived beyond the tutor’s explanation. It also gives parents something more specific than “the lesson went well”.

A practical example

Ask for three sentences: what was practised, one example now understood and the next question to try. A younger pupil can dictate or draw instead. The tutor can correct a misunderstanding while keeping the wording recognisably the learner’s.

At the next session, begin with that question rather than a completely unrelated worksheet. If the handover is vague, narrow the lesson goal. Harborne enquiries should confirm the practical arrangement with Teaching Success in Smethwick or online before relying on a weekly travel plan.

Make feedback a two-way exchange

After a lesson, ask the learner to finish two statements: “The explanation that helped was…” and “I still need to check…”. Let the tutor add the next independent task alongside those observations.

Compare that note with the next attempt. If the learner found a demonstration reassuring but cannot use the idea, the next lesson can address the gap without dismissing their experience. Keep feedback brief and specific so it helps plan teaching rather than becoming a general report of whether the session felt good.
