Make the language of a word problem visible

A maths word problem can fail at the reading stage even when the calculation is within reach. Separating the story from the arithmetic shows where support belongs.

A practical example

Use a fictional shop problem: three notebooks cost £2 each and a pen costs £1. Ask the pupil to describe what is bought and draw the groups before calculating £7. Change only the wording on the next attempt, keeping the mathematical structure similar.

Discuss unfamiliar vocabulary without implying anything about a family’s home language or background. If the arithmetic is secure once the question is explained, the next goal may be interpreting instructions. Cape Hill families should confirm online or Smethwick lesson arrangements directly.

Draw the story before selecting the arithmetic

Use the fictional problem “There are 18 books; 6 are on a shelf and the rest are in a box”. Ask the child to draw or label the two groups before writing 18 − 6 = 12.

Now change the question to ask how many books there are altogether when the shelf and box quantities are given. The vocabulary may stay similar while the unknown changes. A diagram can reveal whether the obstacle is interpreting the story or carrying out the calculation, helping the tutor choose the right explanation.
