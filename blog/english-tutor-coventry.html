September 2026: Keep the First English Page, Even If It Is Messy

The first piece of English writing this term is valuable because it shows what the student does before corrections and rehearsed answers accumulate. Bring the original page to Mr Olu's trial lesson rather than copying it out neatly.

  • Read the question beside the answer: A well-written paragraph can still lose marks if it answers a nearby question instead.
  • Underline the claim: If the main idea is hard to find, organisation may be the first target.
  • Box the evidence: Check whether quotations are selected precisely and then explained, rather than dropped into the paragraph.
  • Listen to the spoken version: A stronger verbal answer can show that vocabulary or sentence control is blocking written performance.

After four weeks, compare a fresh response to the original for clearer ideas, better evidence and less prompting.
