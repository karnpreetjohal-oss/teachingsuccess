What Year 8 Maths covers

Year 8 deepens the algebra started in Year 7. Students expand and factorise brackets, plot and interpret linear graphs, use percentage multipliers for increase and decrease, work with angles and parallel lines, and extend ratio and proportion into more demanding problems.

Where Year 8 students most often need help

By Year 8, small unaddressed weaknesses compound: a student who never quite secured directed numbers or basic algebra now finds graphs and equations genuinely hard. Careless sign errors in algebra are a very common mark-loser.

The assessment that shapes Year 8

Year 8 assessments feed directly into GCSE tier and set decisions in many schools. Because there is no national exam, it is an easy year to coast — which is exactly why steady, targeted support here prevents a difficult Year 9.

What a weekly Year 8 Maths plan looks like

  • Expanding and factorising single brackets accurately
  • Plotting and reading straight-line graphs
  • Percentage change using multipliers
  • Angle rules, including parallel lines and basic reasoning

Looking ahead

Year 8 is the last full year before many schools begin GCSE content in Year 9, so consolidating algebra now pays off directly.
