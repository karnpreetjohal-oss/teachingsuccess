Three useful projects to bring to a lesson

Training becomes more memorable when every tool solves a real problem. Bring one unfinished task and leave with both a stronger result and a repeatable method.

  • An interview presentation: Turn a loose brief into a five-slide story with a clear opening, evidence and recommendation.
  • A monthly spreadsheet: Clean inconsistent entries, add reliable formulas and create a chart that answers one management question.
  • A professional document: Build a CV, report or letter with styles, spacing and headings that remain consistent when the content changes.

How the training works

The first session is a free trial and a skills check: what you can already do, what you need, and what the job, course or goal in front of you actually requires. From there you get a short, structured plan — usually weekly one-hour sessions with small practice tasks between them, built around real documents and spreadsheets from your own work or life wherever possible.

September 2026: turn one real task into an Office skills check

September often arrives with new reports, inductions and requests marked “quick” that are rarely quick. For someone comparing Microsoft Office training in Birmingham, a useful starting check is to complete one small job from beginning to end and notice exactly where the process slows down.

  • Outlook: Find the request, identify the deadline and file the message so the action is easy to recover.
  • Excel: Enter or clean the figures, calculate one result and add a check that would reveal a mistake.
  • Word: Write the short report with real heading styles so later edits do not break the layout.
  • PowerPoint: Present the conclusion in three clear slides, then explain it without reading the screen.

Write down the step that needed guessing, repeated clicking or outside help. That becomes the first training goal; it is much more useful than starting at page one of a manual.

What Progress Looks Like

Useful progress is visible in completed work: a document that keeps its formatting, a spreadsheet that updates correctly, a presentation that is easy to follow, or an inbox routine that makes actions easier to find. At the end of each training block, repeat a task from the first session with less support and compare the result.
