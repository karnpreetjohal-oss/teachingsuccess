What should the first maths lesson reveal?

A useful trial lesson does not begin by racing through a worksheet. It identifies what the pupil can do independently, where the first dependable method breaks down and whether the main barrier is knowledge, recall, language, pace or confidence.

Bring a recent school test, report or exercise-book page. One carefully chosen error often tells us more than a long list of topics: a wrong fraction answer may come from weak multiplication facts, an algebra error may begin with negative numbers, and a lost GCSE mark may be caused by interpretation rather than calculation.

How maths support changes by school stage

  • KS2: Build number facts, place value, written methods and the language needed to explain a solution. See KS2 Maths tuition.
  • KS3: Repair arithmetic gaps while developing algebra, ratio, graphs and independent problem solving before GCSE choices narrow the timetable.
  • GCSE: Match teaching to Foundation or Higher tier, exam board and target grade, then combine topic repair with timed paper decisions. Start with the free GCSE Maths diagnostic test.
  • A-Level: Connect pure, statistics and mechanics so methods remain usable when a question combines several ideas. Explore A-Level Maths tuition.

A four-week plan parents can check

Progress should be visible without promising instant grade changes. The first month can use one small measure throughout so parents and pupils can see whether the teaching is transferring.

  • Week 1: Record a short baseline and identify one prerequisite gap behind the current school topic.
  • Week 2: Teach the method, compare worked examples and remove prompts gradually.
  • Week 3: Mix the skill with older topics so the pupil must decide which method to use.
  • Week 4: Repeat a comparable check and report what improved, what remains fragile and what comes next.

Questions to ask before choosing a maths tutor

  • Who will teach the lessons, and what classroom or subject experience do they have?
  • How will the tutor distinguish a missing topic from a missing prerequisite?
  • Which evidence will be shared after the first four weeks?
  • Will practice match the pupil's year group, tier and exam board?
  • What should the pupil do independently between sessions?

Late August 2026: prepare for the first month of term

Use the final part of the summer to establish a calm starting point, not to pre-teach an entire term. The most useful evidence is a short mixed check completed without notes, followed by a conversation about how each answer was chosen.

  • Choose one routine: Ten focused minutes several times a week is more sustainable than one long catch-up session.
  • Keep the workings: Do not erase incorrect attempts before the tutor can see the decision that produced them.
  • Note the new timetable: Bring the exam board, set, tier and current school topic as soon as they are confirmed.
  • Review after four weeks: Change the plan only after comparing equivalent evidence, not one unusually easy or difficult homework.

How to tell whether maths tuition is working

Look for a change in independence as well as marks. A pupil who starts without waiting for a prompt, explains why a method fits and spots an unreasonable answer is building knowledge that can survive a new question.

Useful progress evidence includes fewer repeated errors, more questions attempted in the same time, clearer written reasoning and a better match between home practice and school assessments.
