Practical guide · Updated 19 September 2026

A comparison needs a shared question

Comparative writing is easier when both texts are examined through the same lens. Two separate summaries do not automatically become a comparison when joined together.

A practical example

Use two short descriptions of an invented park, one welcoming and one unsettling. Ask how each writer presents the same place. Select one detail from each, explain its effect and connect the contrast to that shared question.

Plan in paired points rather than writing everything about text A before beginning text B. Keep interpretations grounded in the actual wording.
