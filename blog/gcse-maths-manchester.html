A scale drawing needs one consistent conversion

Online GCSE maths practice can make scale errors visible by asking the student to annotate every conversion. A correct-looking drawing is not enough if its measurements use different scales.

A practical example

At a scale of 1:200, 3 cm on a plan represents 600 cm, or 6 m, in reality. A real length of 9 m becomes 4.5 cm on the plan. Convert to matching units before applying the ratio.

Ask the student to label a second dimension and check it in reverse. Share a clear image of the working during an online lesson. Teaching Success does not advertise a current Manchester tutor branch here; enquiries are handled by the Smethwick-based team, subject to availability.
