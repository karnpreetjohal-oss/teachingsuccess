When siblings need different kinds of help

Families arranging tuition for siblings may be tempted to choose the same task and pace for both. A shared timetable does not mean the learning goals should be identical.

A practical example

Give each child a separate starting question and ask them to explain their attempt without comparing scores. One might need help decoding instructions while another needs a harder application task. Record each goal in the child’s own words.

Discuss whether separate sessions, different activities or another arrangement would suit them best, subject to availability. Avoid using the older child as the younger child’s permanent helper. Confirm the actual teaching location with the Smethwick-based team rather than assuming that a Bearwood guide represents a separate branch.

Give siblings separate learning goals even in a shared routine

Two children can start homework at the same time while working towards different outcomes. One might practise explaining a multiplication fact; another might revise a paragraph so the reader can follow the argument.

Agree a small finishing point for each and avoid comparing how quickly they complete it. If tuition is being considered, describe each child’s subject and support needs separately. A shared household routine does not establish that the same lesson format, task difficulty or tutor arrangement will suit both learners.
