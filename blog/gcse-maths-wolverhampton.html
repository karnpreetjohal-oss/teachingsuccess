Why GCSE Maths needs a structured approach

GCSE Maths contains over 90 distinct topic areas. Students who struggle are not struggling at everything — they typically have 10–15 weak topics pulling their grade down. Good tuition identifies those topics quickly and retests them until they're solid. Random revision doesn't do this. Structured teaching does.

What Mr Olu focuses on in GCSE Maths lessons

  • Diagnosing weak topics using recent mock papers and school tests
  • Reteaching foundation concepts before applying them to exam questions
  • Method marks — showing clear working to pick up partial credit
  • Calculator and non-calculator paper skills (students often lose marks on the non-calc paper)
  • Higher-tier topics: simultaneous equations, quadratics, circle theorems, vectors
  • Timed practice under exam conditions every 3–4 sessions

30-Day Progress Plan

  1. Week 1: mock paper review, topic priority list agreed with student and parent.
  2. Week 2: close the highest-priority gaps — typically algebra and ratio.
  3. Week 3: mixed exam practice on improved topics, identify next round of gaps.
  4. Week 4: timed past paper, measure progress, reset priority list for next cycle.

Which exam boards does Mr Olu cover?

Mr Olu teaches GCSE Maths across AQA, Edexcel and OCR — the three main boards used by Wolverhampton schools. If you are unsure which board your child's school uses, it will be on their mock paper or you can check with the school's maths department.
