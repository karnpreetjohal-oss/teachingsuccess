What Year 10 Maths covers

Year 10 is the first year of the GCSE course proper. Students tackle quadratics, simultaneous equations, trigonometry, surds, similar shapes and probability, building the content that the Year 11 exams will draw on. Foundation and Higher tiers begin to diverge, so the right target tier matters.

Where Year 10 students most often need help

The typical problem is keeping pace: GCSE introduces a lot of new content quickly, and a topic half-understood in class is easily forgotten by mock season. Weak algebra from KS3 also caps progress on quadratics and simultaneous equations.

The assessment that shapes Year 10

Year 10 usually ends with end-of-year exams that act as an early GCSE benchmark, and some schools sit a GCSE module or mock. These results shape predicted grades and set placement, so consistent progress across the year counts.

What a weekly Year 10 Maths plan looks like

  • Quadratics — factorising, solving and the basics of the graph
  • Simultaneous equations and rearranging formulae
  • Right-angled trigonometry and Pythagoras in context
  • Surds, similar shapes and secure exam layout

Looking ahead
