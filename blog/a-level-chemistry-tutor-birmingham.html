Why organic mechanisms cause the most A-Level Chemistry marks lost

Organic Chemistry mechanisms — nucleophilic substitution, electrophilic addition, free radical substitution, condensation and hydrolysis — are a source of avoidable marks for many A-Level Chemistry students. The molecules change from question to question, but the mechanism patterns are finite and learnable. Teaching Success sessions work through each mechanism type systematically with colour-coded curly arrow notation, then apply them to unfamiliar molecules under exam conditions until the pattern recognition becomes automatic. That is usually the point where students stop seeing mechanisms as separate facts and start treating them as a connected system.

Multi-step calculations: the marking structure that matters

  • A-Level Chemistry calculation questions award marks for each step, not just the final answer — even an incorrect answer can earn most marks with correct working
  • Sessions build the habit of showing all working in the format examiners reward: formula → substitution → working → answer with units
  • Mole calculations, titration calculations, equilibrium constants and pH are the most heavily tested calculation types and are practised in every session block
  • Error logs track which calculation types are producing persistent mistakes, so revision stays targeted rather than general

Practical Chemistry and Paper 3 preparation

Many students underestimate how much of A-Level Chemistry depends on practical understanding. Questions on rates, enthalpy, equilibria, chromatography, instrumental analysis and method evaluation regularly appear in unfamiliar forms, especially on synoptic papers. Strong tuition revisits the practical method, the science behind it and the language needed for evaluation so students are ready for planning questions, error-analysis questions and data interpretation, not just straightforward recall.
