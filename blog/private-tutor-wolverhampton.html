GCSE Tuition in Wolverhampton — Maths, Science and English

The GCSE years (Years 10 and 11) are the highest-stakes period of secondary school, and the volume of content across multiple subjects can overwhelm even motivated students. Mr Olu works with students on all three core GCSE subjects — Maths, Combined or Triple Science, and English Language — so families do not need to source multiple tutors or juggle different timetables.

In Maths, lessons focus on plugging specific gaps: many Wolverhampton students arrive in Year 10 with shakiness in algebra, ratio and proportion, or simultaneous equations that quietly undermines their overall grade. In Science, Mr Olu works through both theory and the required practicals that examiners test in the written papers. In English, sessions build the close-reading and extended-writing skills that AQA mark schemes reward.

KS3 Support for Years 7, 8 and 9

KS3 is often overlooked — parents and schools focus on GCSE — but weak foundations built in Years 7 to 9 create the gaps that cause problems later. Mr Olu offers KS3 tuition to help students stay on track, build confidence and reach GCSE ready to achieve, rather than spending the first term catching up. This is particularly valuable for students who have just transitioned from primary school and are finding the step up to secondary level demanding.

Common KS3 focus areas include:

  • Number fluency and early algebra (Maths)
  • Cell biology, forces and chemical reactions (Science)
  • Reading comprehension and analytical writing (English)
  • Study skills and independent learning habits
  • Building exam confidence before formal testing begins

A-Level Guidance in Wolverhampton

For Year 12 and Year 13 students, the jump from GCSE to A-Level is steep. The content is harder, the mark schemes are more demanding, and university offers depend on predicted grades. Mr Olu provides A-Level support in Maths and the sciences, helping students at sixth forms including Wolverhampton Grammar School's sixth form and UTC Wolverhampton work through the more challenging topics — pure maths, organic chemistry, particle physics — that students often hit a wall with in the spring term.

Sessions also address exam technique: how to structure a six-mark physics explanation, how to interpret unfamiliar data in biology, and how to show working clearly in A-Level Maths papers to protect method marks even when the final answer is wrong.
