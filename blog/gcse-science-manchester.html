Practical guide · Updated 23 September 2026

An anomaly is a question, not permission to delete data

An unexpected result deserves investigation. Removing a value simply because it makes a graph untidy can hide an important problem with the method.

A practical example

Consider fictional repeat measurements of 12, 13 and 21 seconds. Check the original record, units and procedure. Was there a recorded interruption or an identifiable measurement error? Without a justified reason, do not silently erase the largest value.

Explain how another measurement or a method check could clarify the result, following the school’s practical guidance. In an online lesson, show the complete table and any notes. The tutor can discuss evidence quality without claiming that an inconvenient point is automatically wrong or inventing a replacement measurement.

Write a conclusion that names the evidence

Instead of “the prediction was right”, write a conclusion that identifies the measured pattern and the relevant data. For fictional cooling data, state how temperature changed over the observed time interval before suggesting an explanation.

Add a limitation that genuinely follows from the method, such as a narrow temperature range or few repeat measurements. Do not claim that the result proves a universal rule. Keeping evidence, explanation and limitation in separate sentences makes it easier for an online tutor to identify which part needs development.
