What Year 6 Maths covers

Year 6 is a SATs year. The KS2 national tests in May are an arithmetic paper plus two reasoning papers, and they demand long division, ratio and proportion, the start of algebra, order of operations (BIDMAS), fractions of amounts, and multi-step problem solving under time pressure.

Where Year 6 students most often need help

The biggest issue is rarely the maths itself — it is applying it to unfamiliar, wordy reasoning questions and managing time across the paper. Children who know a method in isolation often cannot spot when to use it inside a problem.

The assessment that shapes Year 6

The KS2 SATs in mid-May are the focus of Year 6: a 30-minute arithmetic paper and two 40-minute reasoning papers. Secondary schools also use the results for setting, so a strong finish matters beyond primary school itself.

What a weekly Year 6 Maths plan looks like

  • Full coverage of the arithmetic paper: long division, BIDMAS, fractions and percentages of amounts
  • Reasoning-paper technique — decoding word problems and showing method for the marks
  • Ratio, proportion and simple algebra, which many find new and tricky
  • Timed past-paper practice with feedback on where marks are won and lost

How lessons work

From there, weekly one-hour sessions mix direct teaching on the current priority with worked examples, independent practice and a short review of earlier topics so nothing slips. Looking ahead

Beyond SATs, Year 6 is also preparation for the jump to secondary, so we make sure the fluency carries into Year 7 rather than fading over the summer.
