What GCSE English should really do

GCSE English improvement usually comes from separating the problem properly. Some students need stronger text knowledge, some need structure, and some mainly need to learn how to think clearly under time pressure.

What GCSE English should include

  • English Language and Literature can be taught together or separated depending on where marks are being lost
  • Live modelling of paragraph structure, textual evidence and argument control
  • Clear work on timing and question interpretation rather than vague 'revise harder' advice
  • A sharper route from classroom understanding into exam-ready written answers

When this route is the right fit

This route is best when a student can talk through a text well but struggles to turn that understanding into marks on the page.

Mid-August 2026: use one timed GCSE English response as the baseline

Before autumn mocks, one completed response can reveal whether the student needs help generating ideas, selecting evidence, analysing language or writing accurately under time. That is more useful than revising every text at once.

  • Keep the original timing: The baseline should show what happens independently, including an unfinished ending.
  • Mark against the objective: Identify whether the lost marks come from understanding, analysis, comparison or technical accuracy.
  • Rewrite the weakest section: Improve one paragraph with explicit feedback instead of copying a model answer.
  • Build a small quotation set: Choose flexible evidence that can support more than one theme or character.
