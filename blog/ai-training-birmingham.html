Learn a Repeatable AI Workflow, Not a List of Prompts

Useful AI work has four stages: define the task, provide only appropriate context, inspect the output and verify anything important. A clever prompt cannot replace those decisions.

What Happens in the First AI Training Session

Bring one recurring task such as drafting a customer email, turning notes into an outline, comparing options or preparing questions for a meeting. The session breaks it into a safe, repeatable process.

  • Define the output: state the audience, purpose, format and constraints before asking for a response.
  • Control the context: provide enough information to make the answer useful without exposing personal or confidential data.
  • Improve the draft: question assumptions, request alternatives and edit the language rather than accepting the first response.
  • Verify and own it: check facts and sources, then take responsibility for the final document or decision.

A Green, Amber and Red Test for AI Tasks

Green tasks are low-risk starting points such as brainstorming headings, rewriting your own non-confidential text or creating a practice quiz. Amber tasks need careful checking, such as summarising a long document, drafting advice or analysing figures. Red tasks should not be handed to a public AI tool without proper organisational approval, including confidential client records, pupil data, passwords or decisions that significantly affect a person.

The UK's Information Commissioner's Office provides current AI and data protection guidance for organisations processing personal data. Workplace training should also follow the policies and approved tools set by the employer.

Late August 2026: Build One Responsible AI Workflow for September

A useful AI course should not be a tour of prompts. Students and adults need one repeatable workflow that protects private information, checks sources and makes the final work genuinely their own.

  • Choose a real task: Use planning, summarising notes or drafting options rather than a made-up demonstration.
  • Remove sensitive information: Do not paste pupil data, client details or confidential workplace material into a public tool.
  • Verify every important claim: Open the cited source and check names, dates and figures before using the output.
  • Show the human contribution: Edit, explain and take responsibility for the final answer instead of submitting raw generated text.

How to Check an AI Answer Before Using It

  • Names, dates and figures: compare them with the original document or an authoritative current source.
  • Links and quotations: open the source and confirm that it exists and supports the claim.
  • Missing context: ask what assumptions the response made and what information could change the answer.
  • Final wording: edit for accuracy, tone and ownership instead of sending generated text unchanged.
