Who should apply

Tutoring opportunities for teachers and subject specialists

We want families to meet knowledgeable, reliable tutors who communicate clearly and put learners first. Applications are welcomed from qualified teachers, experienced private tutors, strong subject specialists and educators building their independent practice.

01

Subjects and levels

Primary, SATs, 11+, KS3, GCSE and A-Level support, particularly Maths, English, Biology, Chemistry and Physics. We also consider digital-skills and adult-learning specialists.

02

Local or online tuition

Apply if you can teach in Birmingham, Smethwick or elsewhere in the West Midlands, or if you provide engaging online tuition to learners across the UK.

03

Professional standards

Successful tutors must be dependable, communicate well with families, follow safeguarding expectations and provide accurate information about qualifications and DBS status.

Tutor questions

Frequently asked questions

Is this a tutor job or self-employed tutoring work?

This is a self-employed tutoring opportunity, not employment. You choose whether to accept suitable introductions and remain responsible for delivering accepted work, professional standards, tax, records and insurance.

Where are tutor opportunities available?

We welcome applications from tutors who can teach in Birmingham, Smethwick and the wider West Midlands, as well as tutors who can deliver online lessons to learners across the UK.

Which subjects and levels can I apply for?

Applications are welcome from primary, 11+, KS3, GCSE and A-Level tutors, including specialists in Maths, English, Biology, Chemistry, Physics and related learning or digital skills.

Do I need an enhanced DBS check?

Please state your current enhanced DBS status honestly on the form. Safeguarding and suitability checks are completed before appropriate introductions are made.

Are tutoring leads or hours guaranteed?

No. Teaching Success does not guarantee a minimum number of enquiries, clients or hours. You remain free to accept or decline each suitable opportunity.

Practical guide · Updated 19 September 2026

A clear availability brief makes an introduction more useful

If you are applying as an independent tutor, describe the work you can realistically deliver. A precise brief helps distinguish a suitable enquiry from one that falls outside your offer.

A practical example

Include the subjects and levels you can evidence, online or in-person format, realistic service area and the times you can usually consider. Describe any limits plainly rather than accepting every possible subject in the hope of receiving more leads.

Do not submit pupil records or other people’s private details in the application. Any proposed arrangement, responsibilities and fees need to be agreed before work begins; applying is not a promise of employment, a guaranteed flow of students or automatic acceptance into the network.
